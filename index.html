Form Check (Beta)

Takes a photo or a short video and uses on-device body tracking (no server, nothing uploaded anywhere) to estimate joint positions and flag a few common form cues. A video is checked frame by frame and the moment that matters (e.g. the bottom of the squat) is picked out automatically — that's more useful than a photo since it isn't just one frozen instant, but it still can't judge things like bar control between reps. Neither is a substitute for a coach, and each check below only works well from the camera angle it names. Range-of-motion and body-swing checks (Preacher Curl, Row) need a video — a photo can only show context for those. Covers Squat, Deadlift, Push-Up, Bench Press, Lat Pulldown, Overhead Tricep Extension, Preacher Curl, and Row for now; more to come.

Good lighting, full body in frame, taken from the side. For video, trim to just one rep if you can — a short clip (a few seconds) processes faster than a long one, and only the first 8 seconds are checked either way.

      Core Concepts

      What is SPY?

      SPY is an exchange-traded fund (ETF) that tracks the S&P 500 index, a basket of roughly 500 large U.S. companies. It's often used as a general gauge of U.S. stock market performance.

      What is a candlestick?

      A candlestick is a chart element showing a stock's open, high, low, and close price over a period. The "body" shows open vs. close, and the "wicks" show the high and low reached during that period.

      Risk/reward

      Risk/reward compares how much you could lose on a trade versus how much you could gain. A 1:2 risk/reward means you're risking $1 to potentially make $2. It helps evaluate whether a trade idea is worth taking.

      Stop loss

      A stop loss is a price level you decide in advance where you'll exit a losing trade to limit further loss. Planning it before entering a trade is a core discipline habit.

      Position sizing

      Position sizing is deciding how many shares/contracts to trade based on how much you're willing to risk, not just how much you want to make. It helps keep any single trade from causing outsized damage.

      Why paper trading comes first

      Paper trading lets you practice strategy, timing, and discipline using simulated trades, without risking real money. It builds habits and reveals mistakes cheaply before they cost anything real.

      Terms to Learn

      Click a term to reveal its definition.

      Volume

      The number of shares traded during a given period. Higher volume generally means more interest and liquidity.

      Support

      A price level where a stock has historically tended to stop falling and bounce back up.

      Resistance

      A price level where a stock has historically tended to stop rising and pull back down.

      Trend

      The general direction a price is moving over time — up, down, or sideways.

      Consolidation

      A period where price moves sideways in a tight range, often before a bigger move in either direction.

      Volatility

      How much and how quickly a price moves. High volatility means bigger, faster price swings.

      Spread

      The difference between the price buyers are willing to pay (bid) and sellers are asking (ask).

      Liquidity

      How easily an asset can be bought or sold without significantly moving its price.

      Stop loss

      A predetermined price where you plan to exit a losing trade to limit further loss.

      Risk/reward

      A comparison of potential loss versus potential gain on a trade, used to judge if it's worth taking.

      Bring your own AI provider

      JARVIS doesn't bundle or resell any AI provider — you sign up for your own and connect it here. You don't need all four kinds below to get started; add just the ones for what you're trying to do (e.g. only "Voiceover" if you already have a script you're happy with).

      1. Sign up with a provider that offers the kind of API you need (see "What to look for" on each card below). 2. From their dashboard, copy the API endpoint URL and your API key. 3. Tap "Add connection" on that card, paste them in, and adjust the request body template to match the exact field names shown in that provider's own API documentation — the template below is only a generic starting shape, not a real request for any specific provider.

      Keys are stored only in this browser's local storage and are sent only to the endpoint you configure — never through any JARVIS server, since there isn't one.
